# Build vs. Buy: How to Think About AI Automation for Your Business

Every business leader we talk to eventually asks the same question: *Should we build this ourselves, buy a platform, or hire someone to do it for us?*

It's a fair question. The automation landscape is noisy — hundreds of tools promising to "automate everything" with no-code this and AI-powered that. Here's how we think about it.

## Option 1: DIY with No-Code Tools

**Tools like:** Zapier, Make (formerly Integromat), n8n, Tray.io

**Best for:** Simple, linear automations. "When X happens, do Y." If your workflow is a straight line — trigger, action, done — these tools work great.

**Where it breaks down:** Real operations aren't linear. They branch. They need conditional logic, error handling, retries, and context. When you start chaining 15 steps with 4 branches and 3 error conditions, your Zapier flow becomes harder to maintain than the manual process it replaced.

**The hidden cost:** Someone on your team becomes the "Zapier person." They spend hours maintaining flows, debugging failures, and building workarounds. That's not automation — that's a new job.

## Option 2: Buy a Platform

**Tools like:** HubSpot Operations Hub, Salesforce Flow, ServiceNow

**Best for:** Companies already deep in one ecosystem. If you live and breathe HubSpot, their automation tools are tightly integrated and well-supported.

**Where it breaks down:** Platform lock-in. Your automations only work inside that vendor's world. Need to connect data across tools? You're back to Zapier. Want to switch platforms later? Those automations don't come with you.

**The hidden cost:** Enterprise pricing for automation features that may only cover a fraction of your actual workflows. Plus, you're limited to what the platform supports — not what your business needs.

## Option 3: Custom-Built AI Agents

**What this means:** Purpose-built agents designed around your specific workflows, running inside your existing tools.

**Best for:** Businesses that need automation across multiple systems, with logic that's specific to how *they* operate. Not a template — a system built for you.

**Where it breaks down:** It requires upfront investment (time and money) and you need to work with someone who understands both the technology and your operations.

**The hidden cost:** If you hire the wrong team, you get a fragile system that works in demo but breaks in production. This is why we advocate for short engagement cycles — deploy in weeks, prove value fast, iterate.

## A Framework for Deciding

Ask yourself three questions:

### 1. How many systems are involved?

If it's one tool, use that tool's built-in automation. If it's two, a no-code connector might work. If it's three or more, you probably need custom agents.

### 2. How much conditional logic is there?

"If this, then that" is one thing. "If this, but only on weekdays, and only if the deal size is above $10K, and only if the lead hasn't been contacted in the last 30 days" — that's agent territory.

### 3. How critical is reliability?

If a Zap fails and nobody notices for 3 days, is that fine? If not — if dropped leads or missed follow-ups cost you real money — you need something more robust than a no-code chain.

## Our Recommendation

Start with the simplest tool that works. If Zapier handles it, great. Don't over-engineer.

But when you find yourself duct-taping workflows across 4 tools, maintaining a spreadsheet of "automations that sometimes break," and spending Friday afternoons debugging notification triggers — that's when it's time to invest in purpose-built agents.

The goal isn't to use the fanciest technology. The goal is to make your operations **reliable, consistent, and invisible** — so your team can focus on work that actually requires a human.
