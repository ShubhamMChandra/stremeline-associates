# 5 Workflows Every SMB Should Automate First

You know automation could help. But with a dozen tools, limited bandwidth, and a team that's already stretched thin — where do you actually start?

After working with dozens of SMBs, we've found that the same five workflows come up again and again. They're high-frequency, repetitive, and don't require deep judgment calls — which makes them perfect for AI agents.

## 1. Lead Intake and Routing

**The manual version:** Someone fills out a form. You get an email notification. You open your CRM, create a record, maybe look up the company, then assign it to a rep. This takes 5–10 minutes per lead — and that's if you do it right away.

**The automated version:** An AI agent captures the submission, validates the email, enriches the company data (size, industry, tech stack), scores the lead, and routes it to the right rep — all within seconds.

**Why this one first:** It directly impacts revenue. Every minute a lead sits unworked is a minute your competitor is responding.

## 2. Client Onboarding

**The manual version:** After a deal closes, someone has to create accounts, send welcome emails, set up project spaces, schedule kickoff calls, and share documentation. It's a checklist — and checklists get dropped.

**The automated version:** The agent detects a deal marked "Closed Won" in your CRM, triggers the onboarding sequence automatically. Accounts created, emails sent, calendar invites out, docs shared — no one has to remember anything.

**Why this one first:** Onboarding sets the tone for the entire client relationship. Doing it consistently builds trust from day one.

## 3. Invoice Follow-Up

**The manual version:** You check your accounting tool, find overdue invoices, draft a polite-but-firm email, and send it. Repeat every week. It feels awkward, so it gets deprioritized — and cash flow suffers.

**The automated version:** An agent monitors payment status and sends escalating reminders on a schedule. Friendly nudge at 3 days overdue, firmer follow-up at 7, and a flag to you at 14 with context.

**Why this one first:** Cash flow is oxygen. Automating collections removes the emotional friction and keeps money moving.

## 4. Daily Reporting

**The manual version:** Someone logs into 3–4 tools each morning, pulls numbers, pastes them into a spreadsheet or Slack message, and formats it. It takes 20–30 minutes and the data is already stale by the time anyone reads it.

**The automated version:** An agent pulls key metrics from your tools overnight and delivers a formatted summary to Slack or email before your team starts work. Always fresh, always consistent.

**Why this one first:** Decisions are only as good as the data behind them. Automated reporting means your team starts every day with clarity.

## 5. Meeting Notes and Action Items

**The manual version:** Someone takes notes during a call, tries to capture action items, and shares them after. Half the context gets lost. Follow-ups fall through.

**The automated version:** An agent joins the call (or processes the transcript), extracts key decisions and action items, formats them, and posts them to your project tool or CRM — tagged and assigned.

**Why this one first:** Meetings are expensive. If the outputs don't get captured and acted on, you're paying for conversations that go nowhere.

## The Pattern

Notice what these five workflows have in common:

- They happen **frequently** (daily or weekly)
- They follow a **predictable pattern**
- They don't require **creative judgment**
- Dropping them has **real consequences**

That's the automation sweet spot. Start here, prove the ROI, then expand.
